Run a diagnostic single-core TLB-restore experiment with an intentionally oversized
source-side and target-side L1 TLB (1024 entries) to test whether the remaining
misses in the restored gem5 run are primarily caused by displacement from the
resume-side transient.

Purpose:
- regenerate the single-core `tlb_resident_rw` lineage under a 1024-entry WormCache
  ITLB/DTLB configuration
- extend functional warming population from `0.05s` to `1s`
- convert the fresh `snapshot_0` through the canonical `.gem`-based convert-single
  path with a 1024-entry TLB helper cap
- compare cold vs restored gem5 AtomicSimpleCPU runs at `100000` instructions with
  `ITB=1024` and `DTB=1024`
- preserve the outcome because the residual misses remain materially non-zero even
  under this favorable geometry

What this package should let us recover later:
- the exact source-side MMU occupancy seen in the fresh snapshot
- the generated `mmu-cpu0.cpt` sidecar produced by convert-single
- the cold and restored gem5 counters for the 1024-entry diagnostic
- the reasoning why this result points to a resume/transient and/or translation-
  semantics issue rather than a simple lack of source-side TLB capacity
