disk_append_log is the second disk-behavior hardening target for restored gem5 full-system validation.

Intent:
- Validate an append-heavy logging pattern that is closer to commitlog-style
  workload behavior than the one-shot disk smoke test.
- Keep the guest workload self-checking and deterministic so success/failure
  can be judged from existing trusted branch-trace evidence.
- Strengthen confidence before running heavier services with substantial disk
  activity and logging.

Behavior:
- The guest binary creates its own parent directory under `/var/tmp`.
- It rejects `tmpfs`/`ramfs` targets.
- It announces `READY_FOR_CHECKPOINT`, spins in a deterministic warmup window,
  then performs:
  - create/truncate the log file
  - append ordered records with sequence numbers and checksums
  - periodic `fsync(fd)` while the append stream is in flight
  - a close-and-reopen in append mode midway through the stream
  - parent-directory `fsync`
  - `sync()`
  - final reopen and exact full-file verification
- On success it enters a distinct infinite success loop.
- On failure it enters a distinct infinite failure loop.

Why this benchmark exists:
- Real services such as Cassandra lean on append-heavy, flush-heavy log paths.
- The disk smoke test proved basic restored disk-backed file IO; this benchmark
  moves one rung closer to realistic logging behavior without introducing the
  complexity of the full service stack.
- The verdict still reuses trusted branch-trace infrastructure, so acceptance
  remains reviewable and deterministic.

Files:
- `guest_disk_append_log_ubench.c`
  - the self-checking guest append-log workload source
- `Makefile`
  - builds both host and AArch64 variants for inspection and guest staging
